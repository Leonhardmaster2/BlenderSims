Real documentary footage from a camera fixed on a tripod in the bed of a ravine. It must look like unedited video of a real event, not like animation or visual effects. The camera does not move, pan, zoom or shake. Real-time speed, one continuous take, natural motion blur, the same flat overcast light and muted natural colour as the first frame from start to finish.

First second: nothing happens. The small grey stream runs down the middle of the ravine exactly as in the image.

Then a flash flood comes down the ravine, and it comes from where the stream comes from. It first shows at the dark rock slot at the top of the trench: the trickle over the step thickens, then dirty grey-brown meltwater bursts through the slot and drops into the ravine. From there the water has only one way to go. It is confined between the rock walls and follows the trench around its curve toward the camera, filling the bed from wall to wall, climbing the walls on the outside of the bend, getting deeper and faster as the channel funnels it. A rolling, turbulent front carrying silt and gravel, foam the colour of dirty snow, short bursts of spray where it hits boulders and the walls. Rock darkens as it gets wet. Nothing appears on the open ground above the rim; all of the water is inside the ravine.

Final half second: the front reaches the camera and covers the lens. The whole frame, edge to edge, is filled with rushing grey-white water and bubbles.

Keep it physically plausible and unspectacular in colour: real weight and momentum, true scale, no glowing spray, no bright white foam, no added sunlight, no colour grading, no extra sharpness. The ravine walls, the rim, the mountains and the cloud stay exactly as they are in the first frame.

Sound: a small stream echoing between rock walls, then a quickly rising, deep, gritty roar of water and rolling stones, loud and enclosed, that peaks as it hits.

No people, no animals, no text, no logos, no watermark, no slow motion, no cuts, no camera movement.
