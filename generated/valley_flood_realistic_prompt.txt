Real documentary footage from a camera fixed on a tripod. It must look like unedited video of a real event, not like animation or visual effects. The camera does not move, pan, zoom or shake. Real-time speed, one continuous take, natural motion blur, the same flat overcast light and muted natural colour as the first frame from start to finish.

First second: nothing happens. The grey stream runs over the stones exactly as in the image, the low cloud drifts very slowly, the grass barely moves.

Then a flash flood comes down the valley. Well up the stream bed a low wall of dirty grey-brown meltwater, carrying silt and gravel, pushes into view and runs down the channel toward the camera. It is heavy and fast, not pretty: a rolling, turbulent front with foam the colour of dirty snow, spreading over the gravel banks, wrapping around the boulders and throwing short bursts of spray. Stones get wet and darken. The front grows quickly as it approaches.

Final half second: the water reaches the camera and covers the lens. The whole frame, edge to edge, is filled with rushing grey-white water and bubbles.

Keep it physically plausible and unspectacular in colour: real weight and momentum, true scale, no glowing spray, no bright white foam, no added sunlight, no colour grading, no extra sharpness. The mountains, cloud and rocks stay exactly as they are in the first frame.

Sound: a steady stream and light wind, then a quickly rising, deep, gritty roar of water and rolling stones that peaks as it hits.

No people, no animals, no text, no logos, no watermark, no slow motion, no cuts, no camera movement.
