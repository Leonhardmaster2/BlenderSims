Real documentary footage from a camera fixed on a tripod at the foot of a steep rock gully. It must look like unedited video of a real event, not like animation or visual effects. The camera does not move, pan, zoom or shake. Real-time speed, one continuous take, natural motion blur, the same flat overcast light and muted grey colour as the first frame from start to finish.

First second: nothing happens. The thin thread of water trickles down the rock steps exactly as in the image.

Then a flash flood comes down the gully, from the top. It first shows high up, where the chute disappears into the cliffs: the trickle swells, then a surge of dirty grey-brown meltwater pours out of the notch and comes down the fall line. It is confined to the gully the whole way and cannot leave it. Because the chute is steep the water is very fast: it leaps from step to step, slams into the plunge pools, rides up the side walls and throws short bursts of spray, sweeping loose rubble and grit along with it. The rock darkens as it gets wet. The front grows quickly as it drops toward the camera. Nothing happens on the bare rock on either side of the gully.

Final half second: the front reaches the camera and covers the lens. The whole frame, edge to edge, is filled with rushing grey-white water and bubbles.

Keep it physically plausible and unspectacular in colour: real weight and momentum, true scale, no glowing spray, no bright white foam, no added sunlight, no colour grading, no extra sharpness. The rock walls, the mountainside and the cloud stay exactly as they are in the first frame. No vegetation appears.

Sound: a thin trickle echoing off rock, then a quickly rising, deep, gritty roar of water and clattering stones, hard and enclosed, that peaks as it hits.

No people, no animals, no text, no logos, no watermark, no slow motion, no cuts, no camera movement.
