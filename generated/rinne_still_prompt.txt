An ordinary, unedited photograph taken at the foot of an erosion gully on a steep, bare alpine mountainside on a dull overcast day. It must be indistinguishable from a real photo a mountaineer took with a full-frame mirrorless camera and a 24mm lens, handheld, f/8, ISO 200, straight out of camera with a neutral colour profile. A plain record of a real place, not a showpiece.

Viewpoint: standing low in the bottom of the gully, looking steeply up it. The framing is casual and a little off-centre; nothing looks arranged.

The subject is the gully itself: what German-speaking mountaineers call a "Rinne" - a narrow, steep chute that running water, snowmelt and rockfall have cut straight down the fall line of a rock face. It is three to five metres wide, V-shaped, and climbs away from the camera at about forty degrees for well over a hundred metres, getting narrower with height until it disappears into a notch in the cliffs and low cloud. Its floor is bare water-polished bedrock in steps and slabs, with scour grooves, small plunge pools, and loose rubble, grit and angular blocks collected on the ledges. Its side walls are broken grey rock, wet and darker where water runs, with pale streaks of silt marking the high-water line. A thin thread of grey meltwater trickles down the centre over the steps.

Everything is rock. The mountainside on both sides is steep, bare, fractured grey limestone and gneiss with ribs, ledges and scree. There is no vegetation at all: no grass, no moss, no bushes, no trees. The only other things are one or two small, dirty remnants of old snow lying in the shade of the gully and a fan of fresh rubble at the bottom near the camera. Flat grey overcast sky, with cloud sitting on the top of the face.

How it looks: soft flat daylight with weak shadows and low contrast, the inside of the gully a little darker than the open rock. Muted, almost monochrome natural colour - cold greys, grey-browns, dull off-white. Haze that makes the upper part paler. The brightest part of the sky is slightly clipped. A little fine sensor noise, slight softness towards the corners, real depth of field with the nearest stones a touch out of focus. Natural, unprocessed detail, never crisp or crunchy.
